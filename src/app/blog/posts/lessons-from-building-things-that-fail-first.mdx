## Starting Before You Feel Ready

Almost everything worth learning looks intimidating before you touch it. Waiting until you feel completely qualified is usually just a polite way of procrastinating. Most real understanding begins the moment you try something concrete and watch your first assumptions break.

## Letting Iteration Do the Heavy Lifting

Progress is rarely one dramatic breakthrough. More often it looks like putting an imperfect version on the table, noticing honestly what did not hold up, and making the next pass slightly clearer than the last.

## Staying Humble with Feedback

When something fails early, it is tempting to treat it as a verdict on your ability rather than information about the approach. Over time, I have learned to welcome fast, honest feedback — the earlier a weak idea breaks, the sooner you can build something real.
