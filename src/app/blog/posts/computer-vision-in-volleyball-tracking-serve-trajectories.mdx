## Why Volleyball Serves Challenge Standard Object Trackers

A competitive jump topspin serve leaves the server's hand at 26–32 m/s (95–115 km/h), while a hybrid float serve travels at 18–22 m/s with almost zero rotational spin, triggering unpredictable boundary-layer vortex shedding.

## Modeling the Magnus Force in Flight

During flight, the volleyball experiences gravity, aerodynamic drag, and the **Magnus lift force** caused by topspin or sidespin angular velocity $\vec\omega$:

<div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-border-brand bg-brand-soft/40 p-4 text-center font-mono text-sm text-fg">
  m · (dv/dt) = m·g - ½ · ρ · A · C_d · |v|v + ½ · ρ · A · C_L · (ω × v)
</div>

Where $\rho$ is air density, $A$ is the cross-sectional area of the volleyball ($r \approx 0.105$ m), $C_d$ is the Reynolds-dependent drag coefficient, and $C_L$ is the spin lift coefficient.

## Kalman Filtering Across Net Tape Occlusion

When the ball crosses the white net tape, color and contrast gradients momentarily merge. We predict state continuity using a constant-acceleration Kalman state vector in TypeScript:

<CodeBlock
  highlight="10-15"
  codes={[
    {
      language: "ts",
      label: "src/analytics/trajectoryFilter.ts",
      code: `export interface BallState {
  x: number;
  y: number;
  z: number;
  vx: number;
  vy: number;
  vz: number;
}

export function stepBallPhysics(state: BallState, dt: number): BallState {
  const g = -9.81;
  return {
    x: state.x + state.vx * dt,
    y: state.y + state.vy * dt,
    z: Math.max(0, state.z + state.vz * dt + 0.5 * g * dt * dt),
    vx: state.vx * 0.985,
    vy: state.vy * 0.985,
    vz: state.vz + g * dt,
  };
}`
    }
  ]}
