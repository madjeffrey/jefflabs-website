## When Answers Become Abundant

For a long time, scarcity sat on the side of execution. Knowing syntax, memorizing formulas, or drafting a clean first pass took years of repetition. Today, capable models can generate plausible drafts, plans, and explanations in seconds.

When answers become cheap, the bottleneck moves upstream. What matters most is no longer how fast you can produce an output, but whether you know what is worth asking for in the first place.

## Taste and Conviction

Working alongside AI highlights how much of good work comes down to taste — recognizing when an idea feels coherent, when a tradeoff is sloppy, or when something technically correct still misses the point.

Models can propose dozens of directions effortlessly, yet they have no skin in the game. Deciding what to care about, what to discard, and what standard to hold yourself to remains entirely human.

## Staying Curious

The best way I have found to work with these tools is to treat them less like vending machines and more like a mirror for my own thinking. Whenever an output feels flat, it is usually a sign that my own question was still half-formed.
